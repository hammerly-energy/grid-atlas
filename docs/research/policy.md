## 1. Great Britain institutional map

**The structural point the template must show.** GB has had full retail competition everywhere since 1999. Generation, transmission, distribution and supply are legally separated under the Electricity Act 1989 licensing regime. GB has **no vertically integrated retail franchise** and no muni or co-op utilities in any meaningful sense. Wholesale trading is **bilateral with self-dispatch**: there is no central pool and no LMP. NESO only re-dispatches through the Balancing Mechanism after gate closure, and Elexon settles imbalances at a single national cash-out price.

So in GB the diagram's **shape** varies by **tariff type and connection voltage, not by territory**. That is the opposite of the US.
- Electricity Act 1989: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1989/29/contents [K]
- Energy Act 2023 (NESO, code reform): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2023/52/contents [K]

### 1c. Northern Ireland

NI is in the UK but **not in GB**. It belongs to the all-island **Single Electricity Market (SEM)** with Ireland. The actors are different: **Utility Regulator (UREGNI)**, **SONI** (TSO, owned by EirGrid), **NIE Networks** (TO and DNO, owned by ESB), and **SEMO** (market operator). The NI Department for the Economy holds the energy policy powers, because energy is devolved in NI. The GB 0% VAT measure does **not** apply in NI.

**Labelling:** the market id is `gb`, with the display name "Great Britain (England, Scotland, Wales)". A later, separate market `sem` would cover NI and Ireland. Never label GB as "UK".

### 1d. Lane placement notes

- **Market operator lane:** NESO, Elexon, LCCC, ESC. Four bodies do the work one US ISO does. **This is the single biggest visual difference from the US.**
- **No "dispatches" edge from NESO to all generators.** NESO dispatches only through BM bid and offer acceptances. Use the label "re-dispatches (BM)" so it is not read as US security-constrained economic dispatch.
- Exchanges (EPEX, N2EX) go in the wholesale lane as venues, not as market operators.

## 2. "Who sets my rate?" in GB, component by component

**Pattern to show.** For every class, the **supplier is the single billing entity**. Every charge (network, levy, tax) is invoiced to the supplier, who passes it through in its tariff. Domestic customers get one bundled bill.

**Exception:** large and transmission-connected customers may have network charges passed through at cost, line by line, under contract. Some become BSC parties themselves, or pay TNUoS directly to NESO if they are a CUSC party.

## 3. Proposed GB archetypes

**Principle.** In the US, the archetype is a property of *where you live*. In GB, every territory has the same institutional shape, so the archetype is a property of your **tariff type and connection point**. Map the chained dropdowns as:
- State → **Nation** (England / Wales / Scotland)
- Wires utility → **DNO licence area** (14), plus the IDNO option
- Supply option → **tariff type**

| Candidate | True shape change? | Why | Recommend |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`domestic_default_capped`**: SVT, deemed or SoLR-default tariff under the Ofgem cap | **Yes** | Adds the Ofgem→tariff `caps` edge, which exists in no other GB archetype and no US one | **Yes (1)** |
| **`supplier_contract`**: domestic fixed tariff, and all non-domestic | **Yes** (versus capped) | No cap edge. The supplier alone sets the price. Non-domestic adds the CCL tax edge and full RO through `applies_to`. Domestic fixed and non-domestic share one shape, and class differences go on edge `applies_to`. | **Yes (2)** |
| **`transmission_connected`**: large load, CUSC/BSC party | **Yes** | The DNO node disappears (no DUoS). NESO bills TNUoS directly. Optionally there is no supplier at all (self-supply or BSC party). This is the analogue of the US `large_load`. | **Yes (3)** |
| **`idno_network`** | **Yes (mild)** | An **extra wires node** (IDNO) sits between the DNO and the customer, with a DUoS edge from the IDNO whose charge is capped by reference to the DNO. About 1–2 million or more customers, growing on new-build estates. | **Yes (4)** |
| Scotland vs England & Wales | **No: label variant** | The TO node name changes (SPT/SHET vs NGET), as do the ROS and AAHEDC. Same shape. Handle it in the `utilities`/DNO-area table, which is exactly the "PECO vs PPL" rule in CLAUDE.md. | Variant |
| Prepayment | No | Same cap, a different cap level by payment method, and levelisation | Variant (attribute) |
| SoLR'd customer | No (transient) | Moved onto the SoLR's deemed or default tariff, which is capped. The LRSP is on everyone's DUoS. | Variant of (1); show the LRSP edge in base |
| Private wire / community energy / licence-exempt supplier | Yes, but niche | Class Exemptions Order 2001. The customer may be off-licence entirely. | **Defer.** Log it as a gap. |
| EII / BICS-eligible | No | Changes the levy `applies_to` and exemption percentages | Variant flag on large_ci/large_load |

**Minimal set: 4**, or 3 if IDNO is merged as a variant. Recommended order of build: (1), (2), (3), then (4).
